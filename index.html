Every useful refinance conversation begins with a stated purpose. The source material frames the review around the reason itself: repayments may have drifted from budget, the loan features may no longer match how the household uses the account, lender service may have slipped, debt may need restructuring, or a planned change in household spending may be coming. Naming that reason before making contact keeps the first call anchored to the actual lending situation.

What to gather before any comparison can start

The published path runs in three moves: state what you need, receive broker options, then compare and choose. The enquiry is described as free to submit, with no obligation to proceed. None of those steps works well without evidence on hand.

  • Existing loan details: the current lender, loan type and recent statements, so an alternative can be measured against the loan you actually hold.
  • Income documents: payslips or equivalent records, plus anything that shows a consistent savings history.
  • Property information: the property's use and current position, since this shapes how a lender would view the application.
  • Timing constraints: any rate change, fixed period end or planned budget shift that affects when a switch must be completed.

A concise brief covering these points keeps the opening conversation close to the real situation instead of a generic product pitch.

Questions that test a broker's refinance experience

Anyone can quote a rate. The questions below probe whether the broker regularly works with borrowers who already hold a loan:

  • How will you check whether my current loan still fits before recommending a switch?
  • Which lenders can you access for my circumstances, and how is eligibility checked for each?
  • What fees could apply, both on the new loan and for leaving the existing one?
  • Which documents do you need before a meaningful side by side comparison is possible?
  • What may change once my income, loan and property details are reviewed?

ASIC Moneysmart publishes independent home loan guidance, so any explanation that stays vague after these questions can be checked against a neutral reference before you proceed.

Trade-offs worth weighing before you switch

A lower repayment is not the only variable. Leaving an existing loan can mean surrendering features the household relies on, and a new loan can carry fees or different conditions. Where the loan balance sits relative to the property's value matters too, because a high loan-to-value ratio can trigger extra costs such as lenders mortgage insurance on the new facility. Whether that applies depends on the individual loan and property position, so it is a question for the broker rather than an assumption.

It is also worth asking what the broker cannot confirm yet. Some answers only become clear after evidence is reviewed, and a broker who flags those limits plainly is more useful than one who promises certainty early.

Who this applies to

This page suits Brisbane and Queensland borrowers who already hold a home loan and are weighing up whether it still fits. It is most useful when the reason for reviewing the loan is known but no broker has been compared yet: households watching repayments, owners restructuring debt, or borrowers whose lender service has fallen short. First home buyers preparing for a purchase, investors separating lending questions from investment strategy, and construction borrowers will find the preparation logic transfers, but their documents differ.

It is less useful for anyone holding a written credit proposal who now needs legal, tax or financial advice. Listing material should be treated as a starting point for enquiry, and provider pages themselves direct users to contact the business directly to confirm lender access, eligibility, fees and availability.

  1. Define the review. Write down the reason the existing loan is being examined, such as repayments, features, service, debt structure or budget change.
  2. Assemble the evidence. Collect income records, current lender details, loan statements, property information and any timing constraints.
  3. Set the scope questions. Confirm how lender access, fees and eligibility checks will be handled for your circumstances, and what the broker can and cannot advise on.
  4. Compare the responses. Judge brokers on clarity, relevance to the refinance purpose and how plainly they explain assumptions and unknowns.

Common questions

Is submitting a refinance enquiry through the source guide free? The source material states the enquiry is free to submit and carries no obligation to proceed. That describes the enquiry step only, not the cost of any loan or broker service later, which depends on the broker and the loan.

What documents does a Brisbane refinance broker need first? The source guide points to income records, existing loan information, property details and anything that fixes your timing. The broker can then say what is still missing before a meaningful comparison of options can be made.

Can a broker tell me whether to refinance before seeing my documents? The guide suggests some answers only emerge after income, loan and property information is reviewed. Early responses should therefore be treated as provisional, with fees, lender access and eligibility confirmed in writing as evidence is assessed.
